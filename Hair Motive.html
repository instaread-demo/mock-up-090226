Olaplex is one of those hair-care lines that sounds simple until you are actually staring at the bottles. The numbers feel like they should form a neat step-by-step routine, but the lineup does not work like a required set. One product may be a repair treatment, another may be a toning shampoo, another may be a styling cream, and another may only make sense if your hair is fine, dry or heat-styled.

That is why the smartest Olaplex purchase starts with the problem, not the number. Bleached hair, brassy blonde hair, fine hair that collapses under conditioner, dull buildup, rough ends and styled curls all point to different products. This guide walks through the lineup by decision point so you can understand what each bottle is for before deciding what belongs in your routine.

The goal is not to collect every product. It is to choose the one or two formulas that solve the problem you actually have, then stop before the routine gets expensive and confusing.

How to Think About the Lineup Before You Buy

Start by deciding whether your biggest issue is damage, maintenance, tone, moisture, buildup or styling. A repair treatment is the right lane for hair that feels compromised after bleach, color, chemical services or repeated heat. Shampoo and conditioner are the right lane when you want an ongoing wash-day routine. Purple formulas only make sense when brassiness is part of the problem.

Fine hair needs extra restraint because rich formulas can make the hair look flatter even when they feel soft. Dry, rough hair may need a mask more than another bond-repair step. And if your main frustration appears after styling, a leave-in, cream, serum or gel may make more sense than buying another rinse-out product.

Read the product sections below in that order: first identify your hair goal, then match the product to the role it plays. That hierarchy matters more than the number on the bottle.

Olaplex No. 0: Best If You Already Know You Like Olaplex

No. 0 Intensive Bond Building Treatment is the pre-treatment step. Think of it as a booster, not the first bottle every beginner needs. It makes the most sense for someone who already uses an Olaplex pre-shampoo treatment and wants to turn wash day into a more deliberate repair session.

  • Buy it if: your hair is heavily lightened or processed and you already use No. 3 or No. 3PLUS consistently.
  • Skip it if: you are still deciding whether Olaplex works for your hair. Start with the main treatment first.
  • Routine fit: pre-wash repair step before your main treatment.

Olaplex No. 3PLUS: Repair Treatment for Damaged Hair

No. 3PLUS Complete Repair Treatment is the current centerpiece of the at-home repair story. Olaplex describes it as the newer version of the cult No. 3 idea, aimed at improving the major visible signs of hair health, including strength, smoothness, softness, shine and shape.

It belongs in the conversation when hair feels different after bleach, color, heat styling or chemical work and you are trying to decide whether Olaplex is worth buying for real damage rather than ordinary dryness.

  • Buy it if: your hair is breaking, rough, over-lightened, over-styled or generally feels less resilient than it used to.
  • Skip it if: your only issue is scalp oil, dandruff, hair loss from the root or a cut that needs shaping. Bond repair is not a fix for every hair concern.
  • Routine fit: use as a pre-shampoo treatment, then wash and condition as directed.

Olaplex No. 4 and No. 5: Best Classic Wash-Day Duo

No. 4 Bond Maintenance Shampoo and No. 5 Bond Maintenance Conditioner are the classic maintenance pair. They make sense when you want the Olaplex routine to continue beyond the treatment step, especially if your hair is color-treated or regularly heat-styled.

The key is not to assume the classic duo is automatically best for every head of hair. If your hair is very fine, the fine-hair formulas may be a better fit. If your hair is blonde or gray and brassy, the purple formulas solve a different problem.

  • Buy it if: you want a simple shampoo-and-conditioner pair for damaged, color-treated or heat-styled hair.
  • Skip it if: your hair gets flat easily or you specifically need toning.
  • Routine fit: use as your regular wash and condition pair, with No. 3PLUS as the treatment step when needed.

Olaplex No. 4P and No. 5P: Best for Blonde, Gray and Lightened Hair

No. 4P Blonde Enhancer Shampoo and No. 5P Blonde Enhancer Conditioner are for tone management. These belong in the routine when brassiness is part of the problem. They are not a universal upgrade from No. 4 and No. 5.

If your blonde or gray hair turns yellow quickly, a purple formula can be useful. If your hair is already cool, silver or porous, too much purple care can leave the tone looking dull, smoky or uneven. This is where moderation matters.

  • Buy it if: blonde, highlighted, silver or gray hair is getting warm or yellow between salon appointments.
  • Skip it if: your color is not brassy, your hair grabs purple easily, or your main problem is dryness.
  • Routine fit: rotate with your regular shampoo or conditioner instead of using it automatically every wash.

Olaplex No. 4C: Best When Hair Feels Coated

No. 4C Bond Maintenance Clarifying Shampoo is the one to consider when your hair feels dull, coated or heavy from styling products, minerals, oil or buildup. It is not the same as a daily shampoo for everyone. Clarifying too often can make already-dry hair feel worse.

  • Buy it if: your hair looks dull even after washing, your roots feel waxy, or styling products seem to sit on the hair.
  • Skip it if: your scalp is irritated, your hair is extremely dry, or you already clarify regularly.
  • Routine fit: occasional reset shampoo, followed by conditioner or mask.

Olaplex No. 4FINE and No. 5FINE: Best for Fine Hair That Falls Flat

The fine-hair shampoo and conditioner are smart additions to the lineup because fine hair often has two competing needs: it can be fragile and damaged, but it can also collapse under rich products. If the classic No. 4 and No. 5 make your hair feel too soft, limp or coated, this is the branch to consider.

  • Buy it if: your hair is fine, color-treated or fragile, but rich conditioners flatten your volume.
  • Skip it if: your hair is coarse, very dry or thick enough to need heavier conditioning.
  • Routine fit: regular shampoo and conditioner for fine hair, with No. 9 used sparingly before styling if you need a serum.

Olaplex No. 10: Best Curl Styling Product

No. 10 Bond Shaper Curl Defining Gel is the curl product to consider when wash day already works but your waves, curls or coils need more shape and hold after styling. It is a styling decision, not a replacement for shampoo, conditioner or a repair treatment.

Curly hair usually needs enough slip, hydration and styling support to keep the shape intact after wash day. No. 10 belongs at the styling stage, after you have already chosen the cleanser and conditioner that suit your texture.

  • Buy it if: your waves, curls or coils need more definition and hold after washing.
  • Skip it if: your hair is straight, very fine or easily weighed down by gels.
  • Routine fit: styling gel after washing and conditioning.

Olaplex No. 5 Leave-In: Best When Hair Needs Slip After Washing

No. 5 Leave-In Moisturize & Mend Leave-In Conditioner is the product to consider when hair feels rough after washing and needs softness before styling. It sits between a conditioner and a styling product: more leave-in care than a rinse-out conditioner, usually less shine-focused than an oil.

  • Buy it if: detangling is difficult, your ends feel rough, or your hair needs light moisture before styling.
  • Skip it if: leave-ins make your hair limp or you already use a cream styler that solves the same problem.
  • Routine fit: apply after washing before styling products.

Olaplex No. 6: Best for Smooth Blowouts

No. 6 Bond Smoother is the creamier smoothing product. It makes the most sense for someone who blow-dries, smooths or styles their hair and wants frizz control before the finishing step. If you like your hair sleek but not oily, No. 6 can be more useful than adding more mask time.

  • Buy it if: you blow-dry regularly and want smoother mid-lengths and ends.
  • Skip it if: your hair is very fine and creams usually make it flat.
  • Routine fit: use a small amount before blow-drying, focusing on the lengths and ends.

Olaplex Masks: Best When Hair Feels Dry, Not Just Damaged

Olaplex now separates moisture needs more clearly than the old “just use No. 8” answer. The brand lists a Weightless Nourishing Mask and a Rich Hydration Mask, with the lighter mask making more sense for fine to medium hair and the richer mask making more sense for medium to thick hair. Older shoppers may still search for No. 8 Bond Intense Moisture Mask, so the article should mention that naming and availability may vary by retailer.

The important distinction: a mask is for softness, moisture and manageability. If your hair is snapping from chemical damage, a mask can make it feel better, but it is not the same decision as a bond-repair treatment.

  • Buy it if: your hair feels dry, rough or hard to detangle after washing.
  • Skip it if: your hair is limp, oily or mainly needs buildup removal.
  • Routine fit: occasional mask after shampoo, especially after clarifying or heat-heavy weeks.

Olaplex No. 9: Best Serum for Heat-Styled Hair

No. 9 Bond Protector Nourishing Hair Serum is a good fit for people who want a smoother styling base without using an oil. It belongs in the routine before heat styling, especially if the ends look exposed or fragile after layering, coloring or frequent blowouts.

  • Buy it if: you use heat tools and want a serum that feels lighter than a cream.
  • Skip it if: you already use a heat protectant you love and do not need another styling layer.
  • Routine fit: apply before blow-drying or heat styling.

Build Your Olaplex Routine by Hair Goal

Once you know the role each product plays, the routine is easier to build. Start with the concern that is most visible in your own hair, then add only the step that solves the next problem.

  • Bleached or damaged hair: begin with No. 3PLUS. If you want a matching wash routine, add No. 4 Shampoo and No. 5 Conditioner.
  • Fine hair that gets flat: keep the repair step if you need it, then choose No. 4FINE Shampoo and No. 5FINE Conditioner instead of richer wash-day products.
  • Blonde, gray or highlighted hair that turns warm: use No. 4P Shampoo or No. 5P Conditioner as the toning step. Rotate them as needed instead of treating purple care as an everyday default.
  • Dry, rough or hard-to-detangle hair: look at a moisture mask, such as Rich Hydration Mask, Weightless Nourishing Mask or No. 8, depending on what is currently available and how much richness your hair tolerates.
  • Buildup, dull roots or coated hair: use No. 4C Clarifying Shampoo occasionally, then follow with conditioner or a mask so the reset does not turn into dryness.
  • Styling frizz, blowouts or curl definition: choose one styling branch: No. 5 Leave-In for slip, No. 6 for smoother blowouts, No. 9 for serum-style heat prep or No. 10 for curl hold.

Is Olaplex Worth It?

Olaplex is most worth it when your hair has a clear damage story: bleach, color, heat, chemical services, rough styling or fragile ends. It is less compelling when your hair simply needs a haircut, a gentler shampoo, scalp care, dandruff treatment or medical evaluation for shedding.

If you want the highest-value first test, buy one repair treatment and use it consistently before judging the whole brand. If you want a fuller routine, choose the branch that matches your hair type instead of buying the classic duo by default.

Final Recommendation

For most damaged-hair shoppers, the best first Olaplex buy is No. 3PLUS because it matches the reason people usually become curious about the brand: hair that feels weaker, rougher or less resilient after bleach, color, heat or chemical work. Give that product a fair trial before deciding you need a full shelf of Olaplex.

If you want a simple routine around it, add No. 4 Shampoo and No. 5 Conditioner. If your hair is fine and gets flat easily, look at No. 4FINE and No. 5FINE instead. If you are blonde, gray or highlighted and fighting warmth, use No. 4P or No. 5P as the toning branch. If your issue is dryness rather than damage, consider a mask; if your issue shows up during styling, look at No. 5 Leave-In, No. 6, No. 9 or No. 10 based on how you wear your hair.

Olaplex FAQs

Which Olaplex product should I buy first?

For damaged hair, start with Olaplex No. 3PLUS Complete Repair Treatment. If your main concern is a smoother blowout rather than damage, No. 6 Bond Smoother may be the more practical first styling purchase.

Do I need the whole Olaplex lineup?

No. Most people only need one to three products. A simple damaged-hair routine might use No. 3PLUS, No. 4 and No. 5. A fine-haired shopper may prefer No. 4FINE and No. 5FINE, while a blonde shopper may rotate in No. 4P or No. 5P for tone.

Is Olaplex good for hair growth?

Olaplex is not a hair-growth treatment. It is aimed at hair-fiber repair, maintenance, shine, frizz control and styling support. If shedding, thinning or widening parts are the concern, look at scalp and hair-loss guidance instead of treating Olaplex as a follicle solution.

What is the difference between Olaplex No. 3 and No. 8?

No. 3 and the newer No. 3PLUS are repair-treatment products. No. 8, where available, is a moisture mask. If your hair is chemically damaged, start with the repair treatment. If your hair feels dry, rough or hard to detangle, a mask may be the better add-on.

Can fine hair use Olaplex?

Yes, but fine hair should be selective. No. 4FINE and No. 5FINE are more targeted than the classic duo for hair that gets weighed down. Use oils, creams and masks sparingly.

Which Olaplex is best for blonde hair?

For damaged blonde hair, start with No. 3PLUS. For yellow or brassy blonde, gray or highlighted hair, add No. 4P shampoo or No. 5P conditioner as a toning step.
