Nine Pennsylvania Whoopie Pie Makers Where the Filling Gets Piped by Hand

Laura Sullivan September 2, 2026 6 min read

There’s a special kind of joy in stumbling onto a little bakery in Pennsylvania and realizing the whoopie pie in your hand was made by someone who truly cares. The soft cake, the cloud of filling piped by hand, the smell of sugar hanging in the air – it stays with you long after the last bite.

Some of the best treats hide in plain sight, tucked into farmers markets and country villages. Here are nine places worth the drive when your sweet tooth comes calling.

1. Bird-in-Hand Bake Shop

Ask anyone in Lancaster County where to find a whoopie pie that tastes like it came from a grandmother’s kitchen, and you’ll hear this shop’s name again and again. The treats here feel like they were made by someone who genuinely loves feeding people, not by a machine on a conveyor belt.

Their jumbo version stacks two large layers of home-baked cake around a generous swipe of extra smooth, creamy filling. Look closely at a fresh one and you might catch the subtle imprint of the spatula that lovingly spread it.

That little detail says everything. Nothing about these is rushed or mass-produced, and the flavor carries all the warmth of a place that has been perfecting the craft for years.

2. Flying Monkey Bakery

Tucked inside the buzzing chaos of Philadelphia’s Reading Terminal Market, this bakery has a name as fun as its treats. Throwback-style desserts fill the cases, and the whoopie pies are the stars of the show.

What makes this spot stand out is that you can actually catch the filling being piped in by hand, right there behind the counter. There’s something honest about watching your dessert come together in real time.

Owner Elizabeth Halen has built a loyal following for these creamy, cake-soft creations. If you want proof that hand-made still beats factory-made, this is your stop.

3. Kitchen Kettle Village Bake Shop

Step into this artisan village and you’re stepping into a slice of history. Since 1954, the community here has celebrated Pennsylvania Dutch food culture, and the bake shop keeps that spirit alive with every batch.

The whoopie pies carry a nostalgic energy, the kind that reminds you of church picnics and family reunions. Surrounded by handcrafted goods of every kind, it would feel almost wrong to make these treats any way but by hand.

Wandering the village while nibbling one is half the fun. The setting adds flavor you can’t quite put into words, but you’ll taste it anyway.

4. Hometown Kitchen Bakery

Some bakeries chase the polished, uniform look of a big brand. Not this one.

Their whole point is producing whoopie pies that taste like something made in someone’s kitchen rather than a commercial facility.

The chocolate cake layers come out dense and rich, with a cream filling that lands sweet without ever tipping into too-much territory. That balance is hard to hit unless a real person is watching every step.

You can feel the personal touch in the texture and the care. It’s the kind of treat that makes you slow down and enjoy every bite instead of rushing to the last one.

5. Country Hill Amish Bakery

Behind these whoopie pies is a secret worth talking about. The wife of owner Elmer Riehl has a unique technique for whipping up the sweet cream that goes inside, and that method makes all the difference.

The filling turns out whipped and airy, yet it holds enough structure to suspend each cakey disk perfectly in place. That’s not luck.

That’s skill built over time and applied by hand.

Found at the Berlin Farmers Market, this bakery leans hard into Pennsylvania Dutch classics. The deliberate, careful approach to the cream is exactly why these earn a spot on the list.

6. Achenbach’s Pastries

Old-fashioned is a badge of honor here, and it shows. Counted among Pennsylvania’s most beloved bakeries famous for whoopie pies, this Leola staple has stuck with the methods that made it great in the first place.

Being an old-fashioned establishment in the heart of Lancaster County means the baking still leans on traditional, manual preparation. The filling gets piped, the cakes get stacked, and the recipe stays true.

Fans keep coming back because these never disappoint. When a place has been doing something this long, they’ve earned the trust of every hungry customer who walks through the door.

7. Stoltzfus Family Foods

Craving Lancaster flavor without leaving the Main Line? This market spot in Ardmore brings the countryside straight to the suburbs.

Their whoopie pies are baked in-house, not shipped in from somewhere far away.

Making treats on-site for a market crowd usually means smaller batches and a hands-on approach to putting each one together. That’s a good thing for anyone who wants their dessert made with attention rather than volume.

The result is a whoopie pie that carries real Lancaster character. It’s a taste of tradition, conveniently closer to Philadelphia than you’d expect.

8. The Dutch Country Bakery

Wander through the Bristol Amish Market and you’ll find this vendor turning out a whole range of traditional Pennsylvania Dutch desserts. The whoopie pies fit right in with that heritage lineup.

Amish markets have a well-earned reputation for time-honored, manual methods, and that reputation carries into every treat here. No shortcuts, just the kind of care that gets passed down through generations.

If you appreciate desserts made the old way, this booth delivers. It’s proof that some traditions are worth keeping exactly as they are.

9. Beiler’s Bakery

Back inside Reading Terminal Market sits another favorite, this one famous for its traditional Amish confections. The whoopie pies here honor a recipe passed down through the Amish community.

Sharing space with other artisanal food vendors sets a high bar, and Beiler’s meets it with handmade quality that regulars swear by. The pillowy filling and soft cake speak to careful, traditional preparation.

Grabbing one while exploring the market is a Philadelphia rite of passage. It’s a sweet reminder that the best treats often come from the smallest, most dedicated hands.

Nine stops, one delicious truth: a whoopie pie always tastes better when someone piped that filling by hand. Whether you’re deep in Lancaster County or weaving through a city market, these makers prove that a little love goes a long way.

Pack your appetite and start planning your route.
