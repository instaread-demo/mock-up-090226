Lady A's Charles Kelley is giving people a look at just how far he's come in his sobriety journey.

The singer, 44, shared a before-and-after photo with his wife, Cassie McConnell Kelley, on Instagram Monday (Aug. 31), pairing a recent photo of the couple with an older picture from when he was in active addiction.

Charles' post prompted messages of support from several other country stars, while he reflected on how much his life has changed since he stopped drinking.

Sobriety Before-and-After

In the newer photo, Charles and Cassie smile in sunglasses while spending time on the water. He then directs people to swipe to see the older image.

Read More: These Country Stars Are Better Because They Got Sober

"My best filter is just...not drinking," Charles wrote in the caption. "Swipe to see my before my wife asked me if I ever look at a photo of myself before I was sober and think 'who is that guy?' This is the one."

Charles says the difference is about much more than looking healthier.

"I could go on about the many blessings that have come from putting it down way more than looking healthier, but it's a nice reminder of a place I don't wanna go back to," he wrote.

Several country artists offered their support in the comments. Ruston Kelly called the photo "a good reminder," while Shane McAnally thanked Charles for sharing it. Russell Dickerson also left a muscle emoji in support.

Cassie Helped Charles Get Sober

Charles has previously been open about how his alcohol addiction affected his life and the role his wife played in his recovery. Charles and Cassie married in 2009.

Read More: Lady A's Charles Kelley, Wife Cassie Welcome Their Second Child

Lady A postponed its Request Line Tour in 2022 when Charles entered a rehabilitation facility in Utah for alcohol addiction treatment.

In 2025, he told People that Cassie was his primary motivation for getting sober.

She's the reason that I got sober. She had so much grace for me and went along through the journey with me doing all the work, and I could go so far as to say there's no way she would've stayed with me if I had not gotten sober.

Charles Kelley Turned Sobriety Into Music

Charles has also channeled his sobriety into his solo music.

His 2025 album Songs for a New Moon explores finding his way back to himself, blending his country sensibilities with 1980s-inspired synth sounds.

18 Country Artists Who Are Sober

Although country music is known for its raucous drinking songs, several artists have sworn off the stuff completely. Be it a lifestyle change or a life-saving decision, each one of these artists can say that that their lives have changed for the better since giving up the bottle. They are sober and proud.

Erika Goldring, Getty Images

Ashley McBryde

While many people knew that Ashley McBryde enjoyed a good drink, hardly anyone knew that she was kicking the habit to the curb. She quietly cut out alcohol in 2022 after realizing her dependency on it, but didn’t really say anything until she had passed a year of sobriety. Her reason was simple: She didn’t want to go public with something she might fail at. But now, she says sober is a wonderful lifestyle.

Terry Wyatt, Getty Images

Brad Paisley

Brad Paisley was pulled over for a suspected DUI in 2011. The caveat? He wasn’t drunk. He had been out getting ice cream with his kids, who were ecstatic to tell everyone of dad’s arrest. The truth is, Paisley is clean as a whistle. Despite writing the classic drinking song "Alcohol," he admits that he doesn’t drink or smoke. His only vice in life is his guitar.

Catherine Powell, Getty Images

Brantley Gilbert

Brantley Gilbert’s problems with drugs and alcohol began shortly after high school, and he continued to struggle  until 2011. In fact, he still remembers the last day he had a drink: Dec. 18, 2011. Gilbert credits Keith Urban with helping him get sober. He leaned heavily on Urban’s advice and support, knowing the older star had gone through the same thing.

Vivien Killilea, Getty Images

Keith Urban

Keith Urban has been very open about his sobriety. He admits to struggling with alcohol and drugs just like his father did. Although he entered rehab a couple of times, he continued to relapse. Finally, in 2006 — shortly after marrying Nicole Kidman — Urban was urged to give rehab another try by his new bride, and he’s been sober ever since. His one regret is that he didn’t find a way to get sober sooner in his life.

Jason Kempin, Getty Images

Charles Kelley

Lady A dropped a bombshell when they announced they would be canceling their 2022 Request Line Tour in order to support Charles Kelley’s journey to sobriety. After making jokes about his drinking for some time and ignoring the concerns of his bandmates, Kelley finally decided to make a change. One big motivator was his children starting to realize something was different about their father. Fast forward to today, Kelley is more than a year sober and in a much better place.

Jason Kempin, Getty Images

Chris Janson

Sure, Chris Janson can “Fix a Drink” for you, but as for himself, he’ll stick with Mountain Dew. After years of drowning his sorrows in beer, he finally got sober. It was shortly after he met his wife Kelly Lynn in 2007 — ironically, in a bar. Janson was quick to stop drinking and hasn’t looked back since. He details his struggles with alcohol in his song "Holdin’ Her."

Michael Reaves, Getty Images

Jake Owen

Jake Owen says life is so much better without alcohol. The bottle was slowly turning him into a person he didn’t want to be, but it was an embarrassing moment that had him sobering up real quick. Now, he’s celebrating years of abstaining from even a drop of alcohol. 

Jamie McCarthy, Getty Images

Jason Isbell

Jason Isbell has been an open book in his journey to sobriety. He has documented every step of both his addiction and recovery in his music. Like many male country singers who have gotten sober, he credits his wife Amanda Shires with pushing him in the right direction. The couple has been married since 2013.

Jason Kempin, Getty Images

Jo Dee Messina

Attempting to keep the stressors of a country music career at bay, Jo Dee Messina turned to alcohol. But after years of battling an addiction, she had an eye-opening moment when she couldn’t remember the lyrics to one of her own songs while performing. Her manager confronted her, and shortly after that she entered a treatment facility in 2004 and has been sober ever since.

Jason Kempin, Getty Images

Jo Don Rooney

In 2021, Joe Don Rooney was arrested for a DUI after he crashed his vehicle into a tree. It was that moment that put him on a path of sobriety. Now, more than two years sober, he’s taking each day as if it’s the only one he gets. The Rascal Flatts member admits that had he not had the wreck, he probably would have never taken responsibility for his life.

Kevin Winter, Getty Images

Joe Nichols

The dependency on alcohol started at a very young age for Joe Nichols, who says he started drinking whiskey at age 13. It wasn’t until he walked down the aisle with his wife Heather Singleton in 2007 that he tried to make a change. He entered a rehab facility just 30 days after their wedding, but he couldn’t quite give it up. Finally, in 2008 — after an intervention from his family — Nichols made the commitment.

Jason Kempin, Getty Images

Jon Pardi

Jon Pardi not only knows how to write a good drinking song, he has his own line of bourbon called Pardi Batch. But a medical exam in 2023 made him want to cut back significantly. Pardi revealed in December that he had retired from alcohol and was more than 100 day sober after finding out he was pre-diabetic. He made the decision after his doctor encouraged him to make some changes in his life.

Jason Kempin, Getty Images

Josh Turner

Pointing to his faith, Josh Turner says he has never had a sip of alcohol. While many young people tend to rebel in certain ways, he says he opted to stay away from alcohol, drugs and cigarettes.

Emma McIntyre, Getty Images

Shania Twain

Shania Twain is all about a clean lifestyle. In her biography, she talks about keeping her diet clean, so she abstains from drinking alcohol or partaking in drugs. She is also a vegetarian. These standards are also something that she expects from her band and team members.

Jason Kempin, Getty Images

Shay Mooney

In 2023, Shay Mooney gave up alcohol completely. What started as a journey to lose weight in order to keep up with his kids turned into a complete lifestyle change. The Dan + Shay star stopped eating sugar and put the bottle back on the shelf. As a result, Mooney lost more than 50 pounds.

Jamie McCarthy, Getty Images

Tim McGraw

Tim McGraw is the poster child of taking care of yourself. He committed to a life of fitness after seeing himself on screen in the movie Four Christmases, but his sobriety has been a part of his life for much longer. It was around that time in 2008 that his wife Faith Hill pushed him to make a change with his drinking habits. She told him simply it was taking over his life.

Frazer Harrison, Getty Images

Tyler Childers

While Tyler Childers hasn’t said too much about his sobriety, he did touch on his lifestyle change in 2020. After releasing his album Long Violent History, he shared that he had given up drugs and alcohol six months prior. It appears he has kept his word over the years and he even talked about his sobriety at his New Year’s Eve show in 2023.

Jason Kempin, Getty Images

Walker Hayes

Eight years ago, Walker Hayes was a completely different person — he refers to his old self as an "alcoholic atheist." Thanks to a man at a nearby church, a friend called Craig, Hayes began turning his life around: He found Jesus and he stopped drinking. Now, he’s been sober for more than eight years, but he still loves writing a good drinking song and has even found the lighter side of going to rehab, which he details in his song "AA."
