Italian Grand Prix Preview - Cadillac

NEWS STORY
02/09/2026

Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 5:33
Instaread

The Cadillac Formula 1® Team travels to the outskirts of Milan this week to the Autodromo Nazionale Monza, home of the Italian Grand Prix and Round 13 of the 2026 FIA Formula One World Championship™.

The Italian Grand Prix is a special event on the Formula 1 calendar. It is one of only two races to have featured in every World Championship season since 1950, alongside the British Grand Prix. All but one of the previous 76 Italian Grands Prix have been held at Monza, making it by far the most visited circuit in championship history.

Monza's allure extends far beyond the numbers. This famous, historic circuit is known to many as La Pista Magica. For Formula 1 fans around the world, it is more commonly known as the Temple of Speed - a circuit where records are made.

Monza also holds the record for the most overtakes for the lead, with 41 recorded in 1965, as well as the closest finish in Formula 1 history, when just 0.18 seconds separated the first four cars in 1971. Monza's character has not changed over the intervening decades, and that same spectacle could unfold again this year.

The high-speed nature of Monza places a premium on stable braking performance, traction out of the low-speed chicanes and the ability to ride the curbs effectively.

Marcin Budkowski: "The Zandvoort weekend was a positive start to the second half of our debut season. We showed that we can compete with some established names in the lower midfield. Monza is likely to be a more difficult test. It will be our first race at this track, and the circuit's emphasis on top speed does not play to our current strengths, so we need to be realistic about the challenge and focus on executing a clean weekend."

"Our priority is to maximize our package, learn with every lap and use that understanding to accelerate our development. We will be doing all we can as a team to provide Checo and Valtteri with a reliable race car. I'm also looking forward to Colton's FP1 session and getting his perspective on the unique Monza challenge."

Test driver Colton Herta will support the team alongside his own Formula 2 program this weekend. He will substitute for Checo Pérez during the first Free Practice session in Monza.

Every Formula 1 driver is required to surrender two practice sessions during the season to allow their team to provide valuable Formula 1 experience to a rookie. This will be Herta's third session of the year, following his debut at the Barcelona-Catalunya Grand Prix in June, when he also replaced Pérez, and his more recent FP1 appearance in Hungary, where he substituted for Valtteri Bottas.

The Californian is no stranger to Monza, having scored points at the circuit while racing in the Euroformula Series a decade ago.

Colton Herta: "This is an excellent opportunity to experience different aspects of the MAC-26. Having driven on the high-downforce tracks in Barcelona and Budapest, the challenge will be very different in Monza.

"With the car set up for low drag, it will move around more, placing a greater emphasis on accuracy at the braking points and on threading the eye of a needle across the curbs in the chicanes. I'm really excited to be able to contribute to the race weekend and to the team's further development at one of the most iconic circuits in motorsport."

Pérez has raced at Monza in Formula 1 on 14 occasions, scoring points in 12 of those Grands Prix. Most recently, he finished second in 2023. Checo's standout drive in Monza was his second-place finish for the unfancied Sauber team in 2012. A gutsy drive from the young Mexican brought him to the attention of larger teams and paved the way for his career in Formula 1.

Valtteri Bottas took victory in the Sprint at Monza in 2021 and has finished on the podium four times. He also tasted success at the circuit a year before entering Formula 1, when victory in the junior GP3 category sealed the championship for the Finn.

Checo Pérez: "It's always a pleasure to come back to Monza. It has a unique atmosphere and so much history, and it's a circuit I really enjoy. I've had some good results here over the years, both in Formula 1 and earlier in my career, so it's always a special place to come back to.

"We had a good race in the Netherlands and, without the unfortunately timed Virtual Safety Car, it could have been even better. I'm very keen to get back out on track and see what more we can learn against our closest rivals this weekend. We know that some of the characteristics of Monza will test areas where our current package is not at its strongest, so it will be interesting to see how we perform and what we can learn."

Valtteri Bottas: "Monza has been good to me in the past, and I'm looking forward to heading out on track again. It was a shame to retire the car with only ten laps remaining at Zandvoort, but as a team we learned important lessons from the experience, and we're keen to put that learning into practice here at the Temple of Speed.

"Racing at Monza is a different experience, with the huge speeds and heavy braking. From the cockpit, you feel all of it - and it is a genuine thrill every time in front of a passionate crowd."
