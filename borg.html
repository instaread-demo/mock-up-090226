Giger’s Alien — Artist personal 1979 film set diary is back in a new edition

Review by C.J. Bunce

Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 4:56
Instaread

After 15 years of covering Ridley Scott’s Alien franchise, we’re coming full circle today with a review of a new deluxe hardcover edition of H.R. Giger’s personal account, Giger’s Alien, available for pre-order now here at Amazon.  Giger’s carefully written “coffee table” diary of his process creating the creepy concept art and the actual monster creations and set pieces for the 1979 original Alien movie is a must for fans of horror, art students, and filmmakers.  First published in 1979 this is the book that started it all, and Giger did not hold back, criticizing staff members, arm wrestling production heads to see that his absolute vision made it onto the screen.  The success came in the form of an Academy Award he shared with the visual effects team the following spring–an event that led to another Alien-inspired project, Metamorphosis with Debbie Harry, reviewed here.  Let’s take a look at this historic archive, available again this week.

It’s exciting to sit down with a an artist’s personal diary, workbook, or idea book–it’s a rare view into the mind of a creator.  Giger’s Alien is the next best thing.  It’s not a photocopy of Giger’s diary, but it’s his words, translated and edited, filled with specific referenced concept artwork and production stills showing props, costumes, sets, and scenes, some that made it into the final film, and more interestingly, many that didn’t.  And Giger says exactly why they didn’t.  It’s his indictment of sorts of the U.S. and UK way of making movies, with an eye to the business and economics of filmmaking.  Most of the time Giger seems reasonable–after all, it’s his subjective perspective.  And he’s willing and eager to point out crew members and art design contributors who stuck the landing in his mind.

The book is only 78 pages, yet the illuminating insight into a unique artistic mind is still worth your time.

Everyone knows how grotesque, graphic, and gruesome his work appeared in Alien, but now you get to understand his influences, including some of art history’s most influential creators.  Readers will also see that his contribution wasn’t done in a vacuum–all the players in the production were after the same biological look and vibe.  The only big element they couldn’t seem to push through to the end was making the facehuggers, chestbursters, and final “monster” called “Alien 3” appear to have a translucent quality.

Dan O’Bannon, Ridley Scott, Gordon Carroll, Peter Beale, Peter Voysey, Roger Dicken, Carlo Rambaldi–readers will see what it’s like to work with these and many other contributors.

The book will leave you asking: Who since Giger has made this level and shocking flavor of statement and impact?  Maybe Robert Mapplethorpe?

It’s an incredible opportunity to revisit or read for the first time this key historic work in the history of art, filmmaking, horror, and science fiction.  Giger’s Alien is available for pre-order today here at Amazon, available in book stores beginning tomorrow.  And don’t stop there.  Check out borg reviews of all sorts of books and tie-ins in the franchise–there’s something for everyone here:
